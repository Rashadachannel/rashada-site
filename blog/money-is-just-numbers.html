Money Is Just Numbers

Paying by card has a hidden effect. Research suggests people spend more by card than by cash, because a tap does not feel like money leaving. A hundred pounds or one dollar, it is just a tap. We can use that same illusion for good. When we see our wealth as numbers, giving feels lighter, and we give more freely.

Gold is valuable because it is rare. Cash is valuable because we agreed to treat it that way. Governments can print it at their own pace, and the paper itself has no worth.

Every unit of money is a claim on something real: someone’s time, effort, and worry. When we see money this way, giving becomes easier. We are passing on a number that stands for effort already made.

Some people cannot earn enough. They are sick, they live in failing economies, or they work hard and still cannot cover their families’ needs. Their effort produces value, yet their reward is cut short by where and when they were born.

Giving does not cost us a breath, a limb, or an hour of our life. It costs a few digits on a screen. Yet those digits can buy someone’s dignity: food, health, education, time with family, and space for worship.

Money always turns into numbers on a screen. But once those numbers are spent, they are no longer money. They become freedom, dignity, time given to others, quality of life, social connection, and our الآخرة (akhirah, the Hereafter). How many digits does a person really need once they have covered their needs and a little extra as a safety net? The rest is not truly ours. It belongs to the people we can help. It becomes ours only when we spend it for Allah’s sake.

The Prophet ﷺ also asked his companions:

“Which of you prefers the wealth of his heir to his own?” They said: “None of us, O Messenger of Allah.” He said: “Then know that what you spend in the cause of Allah is yours, and what you leave behind is your heir’s.” (Bukhari)

We can only transfer numbers exchanged for الحسنات (hasanat, good deeds) to the Hereafter. We cannot take the physical form of wealth with us, whether gold, property, or anything else.

Spend on your family first. The Prophet ﷺ said that a dinar spent in the way of Allah, a dinar spent to free a slave, a dinar given to a poor person, and a dinar spent on your family are not equal in reward. The greatest reward is for the dinar spent on your family. (Muslim, narrated by Abu Hurairah)

The Prophet ﷺ said:

“Whoever spends on his wife for the sake of Allah, it is charity for him.” (Bukhari and Muslim)

Asma bint Abi Bakr narrated that the Prophet ﷺ told her: “Spend, and do not count, or Allah will count against you.” (Bukhari and Muslim)

So give, and do not over-calculate every coin. Be generous, and trust Allah with your provision. Allah warns against both extremes:

“Do not make your hand chained to your neck, nor extend it completely, lest you sit blaming and impoverished.” (Al-Isra 17:29)

When we die, our wealth stays behind. Our deeds are the only part that stays with us. Money becomes deeds the moment we turn it into numbers and give it for Allah’s sake. Do not wait until the cards stop working.

So use the pay by card illusion today. Invest in your akhirah the way previous generations learned the hard way, by watching land and gold leave their hands. Do it now, with the numbers you already have, which fade away so easily. It is one of this era’s greatest tests, a weightless purchase, and also one of its greatest blessings for our akhirah.
