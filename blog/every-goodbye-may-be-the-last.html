Every Goodbye May Be the Last

Every goodbye may be the last. Every time you hug your spouse or your child, and every time they leave the house, travel, or go to work, remember that it may be the last time you see them in this world. That thought is not meant to frighten you. It is meant to make you grateful.

The gift of gratitude

Allah says: “And when your Lord proclaimed: If you are grateful, I will surely increase you.” (Ibrahim 14:7)

وَلَئِنْ شَكَرْتُمْ لَأَزِيدَنَّكُمْ

The Prophet ﷺ said: “Whoever is not grateful to people is not grateful to Allah.” (Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi). So gratitude is not a small extra. It is the door through which blessings keep arriving, and the Qur’an promises an increase for those who are thankful.

There are many books today on gratitude, habits, and how people can change their lives. Some of them are excellent research written by people who are not Muslim. Books like Atomic Habits or Seth Godin’s This Is Marketing show how much the human mind responds to small, steady practice. Yet we have a book of guidance, the Qur’an, and a Sunnah full of teaching on gratitude. Muslims should have been the ones to write the deepest research on this, and perhaps someone will. Until then, the best place to start is our own hearts.

Gratitude before the loss

One of the benefits of gratitude is that it changes how we feel when a blessing leaves us. If we are truly grateful every day for the people and things we have, and we reflect on them, then when they are taken away as a test, or replaced with something else, we do not feel the pain in the same way. We still feel the sadness of leaving, and that is completely natural. But we do not feel that we deserved to lose it, as if this were a punishment we had earned.

The grateful heart remembers that everything was a loan. It enjoyed the time it was given, and it did not take that time for granted.

Every hug is a goodbye

When you hug your spouse or your child, pause for a moment. Look them in the eye. Smell them. Be happy that you have this time together, and know that this is a blessing from Allah. Make dua for them. Recite ruqyah over them, as the Prophet ﷺ taught us to seek protection for our loved ones. And if it is your parent or your friend, let them feel how much they mean to you while they are still here.

The point is not to live in fear of losing them. The point is to live in gratitude while they are with you, so that when the goodbye comes, you do not carry regret or resentment. You carry thankfulness.

This world is an airport

Think of this whole world as an airport. We are all waiting at departure gates. We do not all leave at the same time. Some of us go through the gate first, and we leave our loved ones in the waiting area. They will still board the same plane. They just have to wait a little longer.

Sometimes we will be together on the Day of Judgment, and sometimes we will not. That depends on our deeds, and on how well we supported each other for the sake of Allah. Imagine someone sitting in the waiting area, crying, because they know their loved one is on the plane and they will be reunited at the destination. Others around them think they are strange. But they know where they are going, and who is waiting for them there.

Look at what we fight over in an airport. A few centimetres of a seat. A corner for a sleeping bag. A house we will one day leave, or a piece of land we will not take with us. When people travel a lot, they begin to see this clearly. Life is a journey, and we are passing through.

Those who have already left

Some of us have already said goodbye to friends, colleagues, and relatives who have passed away. Looking back, the goodbyes that bring peace are the ones where we did not take the person for granted. We hugged them from the bottom of our hearts, and we held them close. When they left this world, we still feel connected to them, even though they are in another world now.

Being grateful for those we have had is part of being grateful to Allah, who allowed us to share that time with them.

The one who never leaves

What we truly need, and should never think is enough, is Allah subhanahu wa ta‘ala. He is al-Hayy al-Qayyum, the Ever-Living, the One who sustains everything. He does not need our effort to sustain Himself. He is self-sustaining, and He is the only one who has promised never to cut off His support from our lives.

People are different. We never know when we will tire of supporting others, or when their kindness toward us will end. We have all seen best friends fall out, and parents and children grow distant. But with Allah, even when we turn our backs on Him, He does not turn His back on us. The Prophet ﷺ said: “Allah stretches out His hand by night to forgive the one who sinned by day, and He stretches out His hand by day to forgive the one who sinned by night.” (Muslim)

Returning to Allah, tawbah, is not only for major sins. It is a return in everything we do. Sometimes a person does everything outwardly correct, yet their tawakkul is weak. They look for friendship in people rather than in Allah. Even when we serve others, we may be seeking their approval rather than seeking the pleasure of Allah. The friendship and kindness we receive from people is a mercy from Allah, who can give it to someone else if He wills. Recognising that is part of the return.

The increase

When we are grateful for what Allah gives us, and do not take it for granted, we receive His increase. That increase can be barakah, rahmah, rida, and acceptance of His decree. It can also be seen in how we view the people in our lives. The ones who demand our time and attention can become a source of reward, when we see their needs as a trust from Allah.

Tomorrow they may be gone, and Allah may give us a different responsibility, another person to care for, or a test we are not yet ready for. So let us be grateful now, for what we have been given, before the next trust arrives. That gratitude is itself a huge part of being thankful to Allah.

So be grateful for them, for the time you have had with them, and for having them in your life. See every goodbye as one that may be the last. Then, when the day comes, you will not be left only with sadness. You will be left with gratitude.
