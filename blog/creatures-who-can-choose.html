Creatures Who Can Choose

Imagine, for just one second, that you are not a human. You are simply another creature on this earth, like a bird, a lion, a whale, or a reptile.

Look at how they live. A pack of lions does not sit down and debate how to hunt. A flock does not argue over where to build its nest or how to store food for winter. Each species knows its way. Some parents teach their young, but even then the young do not rise up and say, “I have a better way to hunt.” They follow the design they were given.

Now look at us. We have every tool and every choice, and we still argue over almost everything: how to eat, how to live, how to earn, how to rest, how to be kind. We have the freedom that the animals do not have, but we are often lost with it.

The Prophet ﷺ said that every child is born upon the fitrah, the natural disposition towards goodness and truth. Then his parents shape him. (Bukhari and Muslim, narrated by Abu Hurairah)

So we are born with something inside us. The question is what we do with it.

Allah gave us more than instinct. He gave us a clear map. The Qur’an and the Sunnah give us structure for almost everything, from how we pray and eat to how we rest and play. Even our play can earn reward when we intend it for Allah.

Allah says:

ﺰﺺﻢﻼ (And We showed him the two paths.) (Al-Balad 90:10)

And the goal is always the Hereafter. Sometimes the path to it is through pain, and sometimes through pleasure. Both are blessings, because both can build our akhirah when we do them for Allah.

So here is the real question. If we set aside the Qur’an and the Sunnah, how will we live? Where will we find rest? How will we become decent and kind when nothing has a fixed meaning and everything is up for grabs?

A lion does not need to ask whether it should hunt. We do, and that is our honour, and our test. Allah gave us the choice, so let us use it to choose the path that leads to Him.
